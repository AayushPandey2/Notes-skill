# Mastery & Assessment Design Intelligence

## Purpose

Design questions and practice problems around the **specific concepts and reasoning** taught in the theory note—ensuring every assessment measures genuine understanding rather than generating repetitive recall prompts.

## 1. Ten Mastery Dimensions

Sample across distinct forms of understanding (never publish these dimension labels as rigid boilerplate):

| Dimension | What the Learner Demonstrates |
|---|---|
| **Recognition** | Identifies when the concept or method applies |
| **Recall** | Retrieves core meaning and conditions without looking |
| **Explanation** | Explains *why* the mechanism or transformation works |
| **Execution** | Calculates, derives, or implements the procedure accurately |
| **Prediction** | Anticipates what changes when a parameter, input, or assumption shifts |
| **Diagnosis** | Infers the root cause of an observed failure or symptom |
| **Discrimination** | Chooses between competing methods under stated constraints |
| **Construction** | Builds a valid solution or proof independently |
| **Critique** | Spots hidden assumptions, invalid generalizations, or broken boundaries |
| **Transfer** | Applies the governing principle in an unfamiliar context |

## 2. Internal Question Provenance & Guidance Fading

- **Internal question provenance (do not publish as YAML clutter):** Map every significant assessment item internally to `concept_id`, `learning_objective`, `expected_reasoning`, `likely_misconception`, and `supporting_section` (`[[Note#Heading]]`). In interactive `/scribe-quiz` sessions, this lets the tutor diagnose *which* misconception caused a wrong answer and follow up directly on that weak spot.
- **Guidance fading:** Apply the progressive guidance-fading sequence in [example-engineering.md](../../blueprints/components/example-engineering.md) across multi-problem practice sets so learners transition from worked examples to independent problem solving.
