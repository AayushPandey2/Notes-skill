# Shared Canonical Teaching Core

## Purpose

Establish one domain-independent theory-authoring architecture across substantive Scriptorium outputs (`M1`, `M2`, `M4`, `M5`, `M7`, and video notes) so explanations develop understanding **gradually** rather than compressing dense technical material into a polished outline. Pair this flow with [theory_quality_contract.md](theory_quality_contract.md) for progressive abstraction, multi-perspective explanations, forward/reverse reasoning, ideal-to-real model progression, claim classification, and question-specific reasoning patterns. Compact `M3` cheat sheets and question-only `M6` assessments follow their own density contracts while preserving symbol accuracy and assumptions.

## Diagnosing Teaching Gaps & One Burden at a Time

Before drafting each major concept, diagnose the gap: identify (1) what the reader currently understands, (2) what the next paragraph assumes, and (3) the missing explanation connecting them. Add the missing reasoning—never another generic introduction or more jargon.

- **One conceptual burden at a time:** Start with a concrete problem before introducing terminology (e.g., an unstable single tree before ensemble methods; outcome → known examples → combined score → probability → threshold rule before log-odds or convexity). Never stuff advanced claims or dense "Core Characteristics" boxes into the opening (move dense characteristics summaries to revision material).
- **Clean metadata & hierarchy:** Do not repeat YAML metadata fields (`difficulty`) as visible body dumps; open with a concise purpose, learning path, and clickable TOC. Avoid deep technical-manual heading nesting (`5 → 5.2 → 5.2.1`) and avoid parallel formula catalogues before completing one concrete worked example (e.g., teach one splitting criterion through a full hand calculation before naming alternatives).
- **Definition teaching structure:** Selectively fulfill five responsibilities in natural prose (not five compulsory headings): establish the need → state a short, accurate definition → translate it into ordinary language → demonstrate with one concrete example beside it → establish the nearest boundary. Bold only the term being defined; use short non-collapsed definition callouts sparingly so the note reads as connected explanation, not a stack of boxes. Separate definition, consequence, assumption, and optional advanced property rather than combining them into one sentence.
- **One hand-worked operation before comparison tables:** Always trace one candidate calculation by hand first before presenting a comparison table; keep every table focused on **one question** (never cramming 7 unrelated columns into one wide table).

## Visual Identity, Headings & Reading Path

- **Separated opening metadata & clickable TOC:** Place blank lines between `Difficulty`, `Prerequisites`, and `Learning objectives` blocks so renderers never merge them into one paragraph, followed by a compact **clickable Obsidian Table of Contents** (`- [[#1. Heading|Short Label]]`).
- **Fewer, question-driven headings:** Prefer headings that reflect real learning questions (*"Why do we need a probability instead of a raw score?"*, *"What does the sigmoid change?"*, *"Why does the gradient contain prediction minus label?"*) developed through explanation, example, and interpretation—never 1-sentence micro-sections, bold pseudo-headings, or identical headings forced across unrelated domains.
- **Spacing, no highlighting & purposeful callouts:** Keep paragraphs to 1–3 concise sentences, put blank lines between complex list items, and reserve `---` for major thematic transitions. Do **not** use `==highlight==`, semantic highlight spans, or `\color{...}` anywhere. Keep the central argument visible in prose, and reserve collapsed blocks for detailed formula breakdowns (`> [!example]- Understand this formula step by step`), extended derivations, prerequisite refreshers, additional calculations, and checkpoint answers (`> [!question]` visible with nested `> > [!success]- Reveal Answer`). Never treat bare displayed formulas as compliant merely because their symbols appear in a trailing notation table.

## Tone, Precision & Accuracy Discipline

Write calm, direct reference prose free of scripted filler (`"We now..."`, `"Let us..."`, `"Notice how..."`, `"Inspect Figure..."`) and unedited self-correction transcripts (`"Wait—check the sign carefully!"`). Flag and qualify strong claims—especially guaranteed calibration, existence/uniqueness of an optimum, outlier robustness, solver defaults, regularization scaling, and universal preprocessing—clearly separating helpful simplifications from unconditional truths.

## Progressive Explanation & Consolidated Scope

1. **Motivate every representation:** For any transformation (e.g., probability → odds → log-odds), explain what it expresses, why transform, what changes numerically, what stays invariant, and how it connects to the model before summarizing in a table.
2. **Build multi-step objectives via questions:** Before giving a full likelihood or loss formula, walk through the case-by-case questions (`y = 1` vs `y = 0` → single combined expression → independence product → log preservation → sign flip → averaging) with a tiny numerical probability example.
3. **Separate training from prediction & declare toy simplifications:** Explicitly separate **training phase** operations (e.g., bootstrap sampling, per-node feature subsampling, parameter fitting) from **prediction phase** operations (fixed model, routing, aggregation). Explicitly declare miniature-example simplifications (e.g., 1-split stumps vs. recursive trees, reusing fixed splits to illustrate averaging vs. training a separate regression tree, whether duplicate records count multiple times).
4. **Separate theoretical results, library choices & empirical observations:** Distinguish asymptotic limits from finite-sample behavior (`(5/6)^6 ≈ 33.5%` vs. `36.8%` limit), theoretical model variance from classification error or observation noise, original-paper conventions from library defaults (e.g., hard voting vs. `scikit-learn` probability averaging; `max_features`), and task-dependent model choices from blanket `"X is safer"` claims. In numerical traces, explain *why* initial predictions take their starting values, what residual signs mean, *why* features multiply residuals, *why* averaging happens, *why* subtracting a negative gradient raises a weight, *why* total loss can drop while individual points worsen, and what the next step recomputes.
5. **Decision-framed hyperparameters, interventions & aligned checkpoints:** Teach hyperparameters by what they control, what increasing/decreasing changes, the core trade-off, and how to evaluate the choice (placing numeric starting points in an optional practical note, not as universal settings). Teach intervention methods (e.g., permutation importance) by the actual intervention and correlated-feature/causal limits. Consolidate core mechanics before extensions, teach metrics from TP/FP/TN/FN counts before summary tables, and align visible `> [!question] Concept Checkpoint` blocks (with collapsed `> > [!success]- Reveal Answer`) with the stated learning objectives.
6. **Closing & Companion Handoff:** End with a concise **Mental Model** (plus any selective closing elements from [theory_quality_contract.md](theory_quality_contract.md) that add distinct value), trailing **Notation Reference** (revision aid only—essential notation must be defined locally first), and reciprocal links to `<note stem> - Experimental Lab.md` and `<note stem> - Cheatsheet & Interview Q&A.md` whenever the active master emits the 3-file bundle.
