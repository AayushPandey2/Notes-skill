# Shared Canonical Teaching Core

## Purpose

Establish one domain-independent theory-authoring architecture across substantive Scriptorium outputs (`M1`, `M2`, `M4`, `M5`, `M7`, and video notes) so explanations develop understanding **gradually** rather than compressing dense technical material into a polished outline. Pair this flow with [theory_quality_contract.md](theory_quality_contract.md) for progressive abstraction, multi-perspective explanations, forward/reverse reasoning, ideal-to-real model progression, claim classification, and question-specific reasoning patterns. Compact `M3` cheat sheets and question-only `M6` assessments follow their own density contracts while preserving symbol accuracy and assumptions.

## Self-Contained Knowledge Contract & Prerequisite Policy

A comprehensive note must be self-contained within its declared scope and learner starting point: a learner must never need another note or companion file to understand essential terminology, key symbols, central mechanisms, core mathematical operations, governing assumptions, consequential derivation steps, method-selection reasoning, worked examples, or essential limitations. Related-note links offer further exploration, practical companions deepen implementation, and mastery companions strengthen retrieval—they **never compensate for missing foundational theory or essential mathematics**.

Before drafting each major concept, diagnose the gap and manage cognitive load deliberately: identify (1) conceptual dependencies before writing, (2) what the reader currently understands, (3) what the next paragraph assumes, and (4) the missing explanation connecting them. Distinguish clearly between concepts that have merely been *mentioned*, *defined*, *explained*, *demonstrated*, and *sufficiently developed for independent application*—a definition alone never establishes understanding, so repair essential gaps before relying on dependent knowledge. Avoid introducing multiple unfamiliar dependent abstractions simultaneously, maintain consistent terminology and notation, progress through manageable connected steps, and **never confuse simpler vocabulary with genuine beginner accessibility**. Add the missing reasoning—never another generic introduction or more jargon.

Apply the **three-tier prerequisite policy** across all substantive notes (keeping prerequisite importance distinct from learner mastery so mastered foundations are not redundantly retaught, while never hiding essential prerequisites inside collapsed blocks or external links):

| Prerequisite Tier | Treatment When Learner Status Is Unknown / Uncertain | Treatment When Learner Status Is Self-Reported | Treatment When Learner Status Is Demonstrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Essential prerequisite** *(needed to follow central explanation)* | Teach visibly with plain-language intuition and a concrete example/calculation before first consequential use (listing under `Prerequisites` or linking never substitutes, and never collapse into a fold) | Provide concise visible bridge and quick verification cue where consequential | Concise 1-line anchor or omit repetitive derivation |
| **Supporting prerequisite** *(improves understanding, optional for main explanation)* | Concise inline explanation or optional collapsed `[!note]- Prerequisite: ...` refresher | Brief bridge if helpful for the immediate step | Brief reference only |
| **Extended prerequisite** *(relevant only to advanced extensions or specialized domains)* | Introduce only where justified; keep optional or recommend further study without reproducing an entire unrelated course | Link briefly if helpful; do not interrupt core flow | Omit or link only if requested |

Learner knowledge status levels ([learner-model.md](../docs/procedures/authoring/learner-model.md)): `Demonstrated` · `Self-reported` · `Uncertain` · `Not established`.

- **Local mathematical vocabulary:** Before using consequential mathematical terminology—such as *scalars, vectors, matrices, indices, functions, summations, absolute values, norms, dot products, parameters, probability, derivatives, integrals, limits, optimization,* or *complexity notation*—establish the relevant meaning cleanly on the spot, teaching strictly the needed portion rather than reproducing entire mathematics chapters.
- **Prerequisite stopping condition:** Stop expanding a prerequisite once the learner has enough understanding to follow the dependent concept and perform the required reasoning—do not stop merely after naming the prerequisite, and do not expand indefinitely just because deeper background exists. Under `strict` source restrictions, disclose missing essential prerequisites honestly rather than pretending an incomplete unit is self-contained.

- **Beginner accessibility throughout the chapter (concept before terminology):** Never restrict beginner-friendly teaching to the opening paragraph. Do **not** open with an everyday analogy and then drop unexplained formal symbols, sets, or technical labels right at the start of Section 1. Across every major section, **introduce the idea and establish its meaning before naming the technical term** (for instance, explain assigning every region on a plot to its closest training example before calling the resulting geometric partition a *Voronoi diagram*; never open directly with unexplained jargon such as `"1-NN partitions the input space into a Voronoi tessellation"`). Keep rigorous theory, mathematics, assumptions, and limitations intact—the goal is **accessible rigor**, never reduced rigor.
- **Teach the simpler baseline before the upgrade (Proportional Procedure):** Whenever a topic meaningfully extends an earlier method, model, or baseline (e.g., XGBoost over ordinary gradient boosting, attention over recurrence, air drag over frictionless kinematics, indexed lookup over linear scan):
  1. Explain the problem addressed by the simpler baseline.
  2. Establish its central mechanism in plain terms.
  3. Demonstrate the mechanism with a representative concrete example.
  4. Identify the specific limitation that motivates the upgrade.
  5. Introduce the advanced modification.
  6. Show how the modification changes the original mechanism.
  *(Apply proportionally: do not force irrelevant historical predecessors onto standalone concepts, and let compact `M3` references summarize the relationship in one line rather than expanding into a full chapter.)*

<example type="contrastive" topic="baseline-before-upgrade">
- **Insufficient:** Mentions ordinary gradient boosting in one bullet (*"classic boosting fits residuals sequentially"*) and jumps directly to second-order Taylor approximations and regularized leaf weights.
- **Preferred:** First shows on 3–4 concrete values how ordinary gradient boosting initializes at a constant prediction, computes current residuals $r_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$ (or negative gradients $-g_i$), fits a simple model to reduce them, and reaches a concrete limit (e.g., uniform step scaling without curvature sensitivity or split-time complexity penalties)—then introduces second-order curvature $h_i$ and tree regularization directly as solutions to those limits.
- **Non-ML equivalent:** In classical mechanics or algorithms, demonstrate zero-drag parabolic motion (or unsorted linear scanning) on concrete values and show where it deviates from real falling objects (or $O(n)$ query latency) before introducing velocity-dependent drag differential equations (or balanced search trees).
*(Transferable pedagogical pattern: ground the baseline behavior before upgrading it; never force boosting or physics scenarios onto unrelated subjects.)*
</example>
- **One conceptual burden at a time:** Introduce one consequential concept or mathematical operation before layering the next. Never stuff advanced claims or dense "Core Characteristics" boxes into the opening (move dense characteristics summaries to revision material).
- **Clean metadata & hierarchy:** Do not repeat YAML metadata fields (`difficulty`) as visible body dumps; open with a concise purpose, learning path, and clickable TOC. Avoid deep technical-manual heading nesting (`5 → 5.2 → 5.2.1`) and avoid parallel formula or method catalogues before completing one concrete worked example.
- **Definition teaching structure:** Selectively fulfill five responsibilities in natural prose (not five compulsory headings): establish the need → state a short, accurate definition → translate it into ordinary language → demonstrate with one concrete example beside it → establish the nearest boundary. Bold only the term being defined; use short non-collapsed definition callouts sparingly so the note reads as connected explanation, not a stack of boxes. Separate definition, consequence, assumption, and optional advanced property rather than combining them into one sentence.
- **Prepare teaching comparisons & meaningful limitations:** When a comparison depends on a mechanism the learner has yet to see, trace one representative calculation or case first. Keep each table focused on one question. Never merely list limitations: for each consequential limitation, explain **(1) the assumption being made, (2) why the method depends on it, (3) what fails when it breaks, (4) how to recognize the failure, (5) whether a mitigation exists, and (6) what new trade-offs the mitigation introduces**—never claiming a mitigation always solves the problem. Preserve source sequence and compact lookup layouts where applicable.

## Central Concept-Teaching Vocabulary, Concept Maturity & Anticipated Questions

Develop each substantive concept to the target maturity required by the learning objective (mapped cleanly onto the unified `0–5` scale in [adaptive-engine.md](../docs/procedures/authoring/adaptive-engine.md))—aiming where applicable for the learner to **(1) recognize the concept, (2) explain its meaning, (3) understand its mechanism, (4) interpret its formal representation, (5) apply it, (6) predict changed behavior, (7) identify its assumptions, (8) diagnose failures, and (9) compare it with alternatives**. Treat these as internal educational objectives, never mandatory headings: **Motivation** (problem/phenomenon) · **Initial intuition** (accessible mental model) · **Formal definition** (after intuition, without replacing it with analogy) · **Component explanation** (role & interaction of parts) · **Mechanism** (causal state transitions) · **Causal justification** (why the mechanism produces the result, never merely describing outcomes such as *"scaling improves performance"* without tracing *why* larger-range features dominate distance) · **Mathematical representation** (where it clarifies/enables reasoning) · **Demonstration** (meaningful worked example) · **Interpretation** (what the result/equation means) · **Conditions & limitations** (where it applies and fails) · **Transfer** (applying the principle under changed conditions).

Internally anticipate the natural questions a motivated beginner would ask for each central concept—*what it means, why it exists, what problem it solves, how it works, why the mechanism works, what a symbol means, where its formula comes from, why a step is necessary, what happens if a parameter changes, when it fails, how it differs from related concepts, and how to apply it independently*—answering relevant questions organically without turning every section into a mechanical FAQ. Connect earlier concepts explicitly to dependent concepts (e.g., distance metric $\to$ neighbor selection $\to$ prediction; scaling $\to$ geometry; neighborhood size $\to$ locality $\to$ noise sensitivity; density $\to$ physical radius; dimension $\to$ distance concentration) so sections read as one coherent story rather than isolated facts. Address misconceptions contextually right where they arise using counterexamples and contrasts (never postponing all warnings to the end), and resolve commonly confused pairs by explaining their meanings, relationships, distinctions, and practical consequences.

## Visual Identity, Headings & Reading Path

- **Separated opening metadata & clickable TOC:** Follow [authoring_rules.md](authoring_rules.md) for separated `Difficulty`, `Prerequisites`, and `Learning objectives` blocks and clickable Obsidian TOCs (`- [[#1. Heading|Short Label]]`).
- **Meaningful headings & logical paragraphing:** Use concise, student-friendly descriptive or question-driven headings (developed through explanation, example, and interpretation—never 1-sentence micro-sections or bold pseudo-headings). Group paragraphs by **logical teaching move** ([authoring_rules.md](authoring_rules.md)) rather than arbitrary sentence counts, allowing natural connective transitions while eliminating empty filler.
- **No decorative highlighting & purposeful callouts:** Follow [authoring_rules.md](authoring_rules.md) and [math_gates.md](math_gates.md) (no `==highlight==`, semantic spans, or `\color{...}`). Keep the central argument visible in prose and milestone equations, with the assumptions and prerequisite relationships established before dependent displays, and reserve collapsed blocks for detailed formula breakdowns, extended derivations, optional prerequisite refreshers, additional calculations, and checkpoint answers (`> [!question]` visible with nested `> [!success]- Answer`).

Write the explanation as a connected lesson. Put the ordinary-language meaning beside the first use of a technical term and explain why it matters to the next step. Choose the teaching move that serves the difficulty. Use a state trace for a process, a controlled contrast for a trade-off, a counterexample for a false generalization, and a worked example before independent practice. Introduce unfamiliar terminology beside the example or mechanism it explains. A checkpoint should ask the reader to predict or justify an outcome that distinguishes two plausible explanations.

## Writing-Tone Identity & Teaching Rhythm

Write in the voice of an exceptionally skilled university professor whose explanation has been refined into a readable textbook: natural, calm, rigorous, patient, precise, and explanatory. Introduce terminology *through* understanding rather than research-paper shorthand, develop causal relationships between ideas (avoiding staccato bulleted fragments or dense multi-step sentences packing three or four intellectual transitions at once), avoid unnecessary abstraction until the concrete mechanism is clear, and use clear, specific, student-friendly headings rather than dense compound jargon headings. Introduce dependent concepts one at a time, letting each important formula or diagram be interpreted before advancing; allow natural connective transitions without banning ordinary teaching expressions, while eliminating scripted filler (`"We now..."`, `"Let us..."`, `"Notice how..."`, `"Inspect Figure..."`) and unedited self-correction transcripts (`"Wait—check the sign carefully!"`). Flag and qualify strong claims cleanly.

## Progressive Explanation & Consolidated Scope

1. **Motivate every representation & step-by-step objective:** For any transformation or composite rule, explain what it expresses, why transform, what changes, and what stays invariant before summarizing in a table; build multi-step formalisms through guiding questions on a tiny concrete example before general notation.
2. **Separate phases & declare toy simplifications:** Explicitly separate distinct operational phases (e.g., construction/fitting vs. inference/evaluation; compile time vs. runtime; equilibrium vs. kinetics) and declare miniature-example simplifications honestly.
3. **Separate theoretical results, implementation defaults & empirical observations:** Distinguish idealized or asymptotic theory from finite/real-world behavior, foundational definitions from library/tool defaults, and conditional trade-offs from blanket `"always"` claims. In worked traces, explain *why* each starting value, sign, and state transition occurs.
4. **Decision-framed parameters & aligned checkpoints:** Teach tunable parameters or design knobs by what they control, directional effects, governing trade-offs, and evaluation criteria. Consolidate core mechanics before extensions, and align visible `> [!question] Concept Checkpoint` blocks with the stated learning objectives.
5. **Learning Consolidation & Adaptive Closing:** After substantial groups of related concepts, consolidate established understanding, connect it cleanly to the next learning unit, and offer meaningful recall or prediction opportunities—without inserting compulsory repetitive summaries after every heading. End with a concise **Mental Model** when it adds reconstruction value (beyond repeating headings), a trailing **Notation Reference** when quantitative notation warrants one (essential symbols defined locally first), rationale-rich next-topic recommendations (explaining *why* the next topic matters), and reciprocal links to any companions emitted under `artifact_policy` in [master-registry.yaml](../docs/dispatch/master-registry.yaml).
