### Authoring contract

- **Source:** Give title, author/channel, publication date, original URL, actual video duration or clearly labeled estimated read time when available. State which content was accessible: full article, transcript, selected excerpts, or metadata only.
- **Main idea:** Give exactly three compact thesis bullets grounded in the accessed content.
- **Breakdown:** Follow the source's meaningful sections or verified video intervals. Explain mechanisms, evidence, and examples the source actually provides; do not just restate headings.
- Link video positions only when verified, using `[03:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=222s)`. Link article section anchors only when they exist. Do not estimate timestamps and present them as exact.
- Keep equations and code only when central to the source's argument. Explain their notation and attribute them. An expanded independent proof belongs in a separate deep note.
- **What to take away:** Separate descriptive takeaways from actionable steps. Mention prerequisites or context that limit applying the advice.
- **What to question:** Distinguish source claims from independently checked facts. Flag unsupported conclusions, outdated practices, omitted limitations, sponsorship, bias, or hype only when there is evidence for that assessment. Do not equate every opinion with a factual error.
- If source text/transcript is unavailable, try available authorized retrieval paths. Produce a clearly labeled partial summary only when the accessible material is substantive enough. Otherwise ask for the missing text; never infer a video's contents from its title or fabricate quotes and timestamps.
- **Follow-up notes:** Suggest 2–3 meaningful Atomic or DeepStudy concepts when warranted, with the project's actual supported single-command invocation for creating them. Keep them as suggestions until requested; omit irrelevant filler.
