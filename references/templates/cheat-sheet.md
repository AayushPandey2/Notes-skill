### Authoring contract

- **Essentials:** Keep only core definitions, formulas or syntax, prerequisites/validity conditions, and distinctions needed for correct recall. Define unfamiliar symbols compactly; do not remove the condition that makes a formula true.
- **When to use what:** Give a small condition → tool/approach table or decision tree. Use actionable criteria, not a vague “it depends.”
- **Quick examples:** Give one or two tiny representative substitutions, commands, or patterns. State expected results when that is essential to interpretation.
- **Watch out:** Compress genuine exam/interview traps into one-line warnings, such as invalid assumptions, sign conventions, indexing, or complexity confusion.
- **Related notes / Sources:** Link the full explanation/derivation and the most relevant authoritative source. Do not include a large source table.
- Default limit: **at most 350 words in the rendered Markdown body**, including table text, code, and link labels; exclude YAML and non-rendered Markdown syntax. Ordinary bullets have at most 20 words. Use a deterministic documented counting convention and measure it in validation.
- Treat one printed page or one to two screenfuls as a layout goal, not a guaranteed rendering fact. Keep code and tables small. A user-requested expanded sheet can override the default cap explicitly.
- Never satisfy the cap by discarding essential units, assumptions, or safety conditions; remove lower-priority topics and link deeper notes instead.
