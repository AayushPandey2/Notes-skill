# Resistance and current in an ideal DC circuit

An ideal resistor relates the voltage across its terminals to the current through
it: $V = IR$. Resistance $R$ is measured in ohms. Holding temperature and resistance
fixed gives a straight voltage-current line through the origin.

For $R=2\,\Omega$, a current of $3\,\mathrm{A}$ requires $V=6\,\mathrm{V}$.
The slope of a voltage-versus-current plot is resistance, so steeper lines represent
higher resistance. This ideal model does not describe every electrical component.
